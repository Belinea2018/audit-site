Chapter: Methodology & Data Collection Strategy

This section describes the research design, data collection methods, and administrative routing used to gather this public interest dataset across English local government networks.

1. Administrative Mapping & Routing

To create a complete repository of all public bodies managing Housing Benefit in England, a two-tier structural approach was deployed:

  • Initial Directory Mapping: Statutory Freedom of Information inquiries were first dispatched to all English County Councils to verify institutional jurisdiction.
  • Unitary Inclusions: Three specific County Councils—Durham County Council, Northumberland County Council, and Rutland County Council—retain direct administration of Housing Benefit within their boundaries. Consequently, their primary datasets are integrated into the main local authority results.
  • Lower-Tier Signposting: The remaining County Councils provided localized administrative mapping, signposting the specific lower-tier district and borough councils operating housing revenue accounts within their boundaries.
  • Central Oversight Cross-Reference: To establish legal baseline compliance, the identical statutory request was dispatched to the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) Universal Credit Service, as they serve as the ultimate authority on statutory eligibility criteria.

2. Submission Framework & Technical Execution

Once the administrative matrix was finalized, each target organization's online portal was vetted to identify its verified path for information requests:

  • Submission Channels: Most participating councils accepted requests via official, published email contacts, while a small minority required manual inputs via localized web forms.
  • Technical Authoring: The baseline inquiry text was produced using the Microsoft Word Dictate feature to assist the author, then copy-pasted directly into text fields and email clients to maintain complete question uniformity.

3. Question Design & Neutrality Safeguards

To eliminate response bias and adhere to strict research standards, the text of the inquiry was designed to avoid leading the local authority teams:

  • Preventing Provider Bias: The OKEachDay service was intentionally framed as a representative operational example rather than the exclusive focus of the investigation.
  • Universal Text Framing: Every submission included the explicit phrase "or a similar service by a different provider". This forced revenue departments to evaluate the fundamental operational characteristics of daily-contact service charges rather than focusing on a single brand.

Chapter: Data Processing & Scoring Methodology

This section details the analytical design and scoring system used to standardize, categorize, and track individual local authority responses within the master Microsoft Excel repository.

1. Scoring System Definitions

To synthesize qualitative Freedom of Information correspondence into clear data points, a four-tier classification system was deployed. Each return was evaluated and assigned one of the following standardized tags:

  • "YES": A definitive, explicit statement or clear policy confirmation that could not reasonably be interpreted as any other answer.
  • "NO": A clear, absolute denial or negative statement that could not reasonably be interpreted as any other answer.
  • "N/A" (Not Applicable): Assigned when an authority holds zero historical records for the provider, or explicitly used the term "N/A" or "Not Applicable" within their statutory return.
  • "AMBIGUOUS": Assigned when a statement indicates that an outcome is dependent on specific conditions, or where a split policy leaves the definitive answer open-ended.

2. Operational Evaluation Fields

The scoring system was applied across three functional assessment fields within the tracking sheet to map local authority funding frameworks:

Field 1: Holds Data?

  • Objective: To establish if the local authority maintains active operational records regarding the OKEachDay system or a comparable daily-contact model.
  • Permissible Score Inputs: YES, NO, or N/A.

Field 2: OKEachDay (or similar service) Allowed?

  • Objective: To track whether the council explicitly approves, or would prospectively approve, the daily check-in service charge as an eligible housing benefit component.
  • Application Rules:
    • For councils with active records, this captured actual historical determinations.
    • For councils with no historic claims (Holds Data: NO), this field captured their prospective policy position if a hypothetical tenant claim were submitted. This protocol successfully eliminated data gaps from authorities lacking historical exposure.
  • Permissible Score Inputs: YES, NO, N/A, or AMBIGUOUS.

Field 3: Other Ineligible Service Funded?

  • Objective: To identify if the council funds an equivalent in-house alarm monitoring system, traditional warden model, or a preferred third-party provider check-in program.
  • Permissible Score Inputs: YES, NO, or N/A.
