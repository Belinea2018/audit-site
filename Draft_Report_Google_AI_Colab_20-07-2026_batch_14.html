### 1.4 Narrative Analysis: Why "Other Ineligible Service" Numbers Are High

Across the granular workflows (Sheets E, F, and G), a critical pattern emerges: **164 out of 287 valid respondents (57.14%)** flag the presence of an "Other Ineligible Service." This means that even when broken down by data availability, over half of the tracked pathways intersect with an explicit regulatory exclusion. Three primary administrative and structural reasons explain why this number is uniquely high:

 

#### 1. The "Bundling Effect" of Support vs. Intensive Housing Management

Many providers package the OKEachDay system alongside broader support services, such as warden visits, counseling, or general well-being checks. Under strict UK Housing Benefit and Universal Credit regulations, personal care and general support are explicitly ineligible for service charge coverage. When a respondent fills out the survey, they frequently view the communication service as part of an inseparable "support package." Because the wider package contains elements of ineligible personal care, the entire branch is flagged as containing an "Other Ineligible Service."

 

#### 2. Statutory Boundaries of "Intensive Housing Management" (IHM)

For a communication service to be eligible for Housing Benefit, it must relate directly to the provision of adequate accommodation (e.g., maintaining the safety of the building fabric or essential tenant-landlord communication).

*   If the service is interpreted purely as a tenant well-being check, it falls outside the tight definitions of Intensive Housing Management (IHM).

*   Because the line between building management (eligible) and tenant support (ineligible) is incredibly fine, local authorities routinely flag these overlapping services as fundamentally ineligible.

 

#### 3. Dual-Funding Stream Conflictions

Many housing associations and local authorities fund daily check-in mechanisms or telecare infrastructure using separate, localized funding streams. These alternate options include Social Care budgets, Supporting People legacy grants, or direct charitable reserves. When a respondent notes that an alternative funding stream is already active or available for a tenant, the framework automatically categorifies the Universal Credit or Housing Benefit path as ineligible due to double-funding restrictions. This triggers an automatic "Other Ineligible Service: YES" flag in their reporting matrix.

## 3. Governing Legislation & Statutory Boundaries

 

### 3.1 The Statutory Framework

The administration of housing welfare benefits within England is strictly bound by central government regulations:

*   **The Housing Benefit Regulations 2006 (S.I. 2006/213):** Governs working-age claimants.

*   **The Housing Benefit (Persons who have attained the qualifying age for state pension credit) Regulations 2006 (S.I. 2006/214):** Governs older demographics where daily contact systems are heavily concentrated.

 

Under both legislative codes, a service charge is only eligible for benefit coverage if its payment is an express condition of the right to occupy the dwelling and it relates directly to the provision, structural maintenance, and utility management of the physical building ("adequate accommodation").

 

### 3.2 Analysis of Schedule 1: Ineligible Service Charges

**Schedule 1, Part 1 of the Housing Benefit Regulations 2006** explicitly details the categories of service charges that are prohibited from being met by public funds. Daily check-in systems and automated phone alerts trigger three distinct statutory restrictions within this schedule:

 

#### 1. Paragraph 1(c): Emergency Alarm Systems

*   **Statutory Text:** Prohibits *"charges in respect of the provision of an emergency alarm system"*.

*   **Application:** Outgoing telephone alerts, digital screen prompts, and automated response centers are categorized by benefits officers alongside traditional analog pull-cords or pendant alarms. Because these systems function as a safety net for the tenant rather than preserving the brick-and-mortar infrastructure, they are excluded from standard eligible rent criteria.

 

#### 2. Paragraph 1(f): General Counselling and Support Services

*   **Statutory Text:** Prohibits *"charges in respect of general counselling or of any other support services, whoever provides those services"*.

*   **Application:** Systems designed to have an external team call a resident back after a missed check-in to confirm their physical safety are legally classified as "support services." This restriction applies universally across England, regardless of whether the system is managed by an on-site warden, a housing association landlord, or a private technology vendor.

 

#### 3. Paragraph 1(g): Services Not Connected with Adequate Accommodation

*   **Statutory Text:** Prohibits *"charges in respect of any services... which are not connected with the provision of adequate accommodation"*.

*   **Application:** This functions as the ultimate baseline restriction for decision-makers. Because automated daily contact does not clean communal areas, repair structural defects, or manage building utility meters, it fails the accommodation nexus test. It represents an operational cost tied to individual tenant welfare rather than building management.

 

### 3.3 Universal Credit Alignments

The shift toward the Universal Credit framework has reinforced these boundaries. Under Paragraph 20 of the DWP Universal Credit Guidance for Social Landlords, central government instructions explicitly declare "individual emergency alarm systems" and "counselling, medical or support services" as ineligible service charges under the housing costs element. Public housing benefits are legally permitted to fund the management of the property; they are statutorily prohibited from funding the daily monitoring of the person.

  • Operational Intent: The system provides daily welfare verification, occupancy tracking, and resident engagement tools for older, sheltered, or higher-needs individuals residing in social housing networks.

4.2 Legacy Hardware Execution (Pre-Upgrade Baseline)

Tenant records and case histories from the initial deployment window track a compact, physical push-button console with specific communication rules:

  1. The Manual Check-In Prompt: The resident was required to physically press a designated confirmation button on the physical unit before an agreed local cut-off time each morning.
  2. Centralized Contact Escalation: If a user failed to signal safety by the target hour, an automated alert routed to the centralized AlertaCall contact centre. Staff members would then initiate a two-way voice call using an integrated high-output speaker and microphone embedded in the device.
  3. Landlord Welfare Handover: If direct voice contact could not be established by center staff, the system triggered an immediate alert to the managing social landlord (e.g., Metropolitan Thames Valley Housing - MTVH) to prompt an urgent, on-site physical welfare check.
  4. Manual Intercom Access: The console featured a secondary button allowing tenants to bypass check-in timelines and manually open a two-way voice call with contact centre teams for general assistance.

4.3 Modern Hardware Evolution (The Digital Touchscreen Tablet)

To align with the UK digital telecare switchover, the provider replaced the legacy push-button console with an integrated, portable touchscreen tablet device:

  • Roaming Connectivity: Cellular data is supplied via a wire-free, multi-network roaming 4G SIM configuration, ensuring operational stability without requiring localized broadband or Wi-Fi.
  • Digital Property Feeds: Beyond the core daily "OKEachDay" confirmation button, the tablet functions as an interactive landlord communication portal, displaying maintenance updates, rent balances, safety graphics, and fire safety messaging.
  • Resident Self-Service Core: Tenants can author text notes, log voice replies, submit satisfaction surveys, and report property maintenance defects directly into their landlord's asset management database.
