Chapter: Case Study & Representative Service Analysis

This section profiles the operational mechanics, hardware evolutions, and systemic deployment of the core service under review. It frames these features against the statutory boundaries of the English welfare benefit structure.

1. Corporate Profile & Deployment Infrastructure

The baseline model selected to evaluate the administration of tenant service charges is the OKEachDay service, which can be deployed as an independent system or bundled alongside the broader Housing Proactive service. [1]

  • System Developer & Provider: Developed by Alertacall Ltd, based at 3rd Floor, The Base, Dallam Lane, Warrington, WA2 7NG. [1]
  • Digital Target Pages: Factual and operational verification is mapped directly via the primary Alertacall OKEachDay Service Hub and the Alertacall Housing Proactive Overview. [1, 2]
  • Operational Intent: The framework is designed to provide automated or human-backed daily welfare verification, occupancy monitoring, and property engagement tools for older or higher-needs residents in social housing networks. [1, 2]

2. Legacy Hardware Execution (Pre-Upgrade Baseline)

Tenant records and case histories from the initial deployment window (e.g., March 2020) track a compact, physical daily-contact hub with highly specific user protocols:

  • The Primary Check-In Anchor: The resident was required to manually press a designated confirmation button on the physical unit before a specified local cut-off time each day.
  • The Contact Escalation Protocol: If a user failed to signal safety by the target hour, an automated alert routed to the centralized AlertaCall contact centre. Staff members would then initiate a two-way voice call using an integrated high-output speaker and microphone embedded in the baseline device.
  • Landlord Trigger Handover: If voice contact could not be established, the system triggered an immediate alert to the managing social landlord, such as Metropolitan Thames Valley Housing (MTVH), to initiate emergency on-site welfare checks.
  • Manual Hotlines: The hardware featured a dedicated, secondary button that allowed the resident to bypass check-in rules and open a direct, manual two-way intercom call with contact centre teams.

3. Modern Hardware Evolution (The Digital Touchscreen Tablet)

To align with the UK digital telecare switchover, the provider replaced the legacy push-button hardware with an integrated, portable touchscreen tablet device:

  • Integrated Connectivity: The updated tablet operates on a wire-free, multi-network roaming 4G SIM configuration, ensuring complete communication stability even in properties lacking localized broadband or Wi-Fi networks. [1, 2]
  • Digital Engagement Features: In addition to the primary digital "OKEachDay" button and two-way voice call infrastructure, the system acts as a landlord communications portal. It can display text updates, maintenance notifications, safety graphics, and picture messages pushed out by housing providers. [1, 2, 3]
  • Resident Self-Service Options: The touchscreen tablet includes interactive tools that allow tenants to author text notes, log voice replies, submit satisfaction surveys, and report property defects directly to their landlord's asset management database. [1, 2]

4. Statutory Friction & The Welfare Eligibility Dilemma

The modern features of the touchscreen tablet illustrate why this technology causes administrative conflict across English revenues and benefits departments:

[Touchscreen Feature Mix]

  |

  +--> Building Tools (Housing App, Repairs, Fire Safety Prompts) ----> Arguably "Adequate Accommodation" (Allowable)

  |

  +--> Personal Support (OKEachDay Button, Well-being Checks, Isolation Reduction) ----> Schedule 1 Prohibitions (Ineligible)

  • The Housing Management Arguable Scopes: Features like the asset repairs app, fire safety alerts, and rent balance messaging tie directly into basic landlord operations. This allows some housing providers to argue that the weekly fee represents an allowable property management cost.
  • The Personal Support Exclusions: However, the core daily welfare call, the "I'm OK" button, and conversations designed to reduce social isolation fall under personal tenant support. Because these features do not maintain the physical building, they run into the strict exclusions of Schedule 1, Paragraph 1 of the Housing Benefit Regulations 2006. This mixture of eligible housing tools and ineligible personal care features explains why local authorities often arrive at contradictory funding decisions. [1, 2, 3, 4]
