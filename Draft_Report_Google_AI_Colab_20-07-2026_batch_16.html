## 2. Statutory Framework & Regulatory Boundaries

 

### 2.1 The Core Housing Benefit Regulations

The funding of rent service charges via public welfare budgets is strictly governed by national statutory instruments, rather than localized political opinion or flexible landlord policies. Local billing authorities are legally bound by two primary frameworks when evaluating claims:

*   **The Housing Benefit Regulations 2006 (S.I. 2006/213):** Applies to claimants of working age.

*   **The Housing Benefit (Persons who have attained the qualifying age for state pension credit) Regulations 2006 (S.I. 2006/214):** Governs older demographics where daily contact systems are heavily concentrated.

 

Under both sets of regulations, a service charge is only eligible for benefit coverage if payment is an express, non-optional condition of the tenancy and relates directly to the provision, maintenance, or essential structural management of "adequate accommodation."

 

### 2.2 Strict Analysis of Schedule 1 Exclusions

**Schedule 1, Part 1 of the Regulations** explicitly defines which service elements are statutorily **ineligible** and must be deducted from a claimant's eligible rent schedule. Daily safety check-ins and automated telephone contact triggers fail several distinct statutory tests:

 

*   **Paragraph 1(c) [The Emergency Alarm System Excluder]:** This clause explicitly bars public funding for "charges in respect of the provision of an emergency alarm system."
