Chapter: Glossary of Terms & Plain-Language Reference

To ensure this public interest document remains fully accessible to tenants, local housing advocates, and non-specialist readers, this chapter translates complex welfare benefits jargon and administrative acronyms into clear, universal language.

1. Core Statutory & Legal Terms

  • The Housing Benefit Regulations 2006 (S.I. 2006/213): The primary national law that sets the rules for how local councils must manage and pay out Housing Benefit to working-age residents in the United Kingdom.
  • Schedule 1 (Ineligible Service Charges): A specific, mandatory section attached to the national Housing Benefit law. It acts as an absolute checklist of items, features, or fees that are legally prohibited from being paid for with public welfare funds. By law, any charge found on this list must be subtracted from a tenant's rent account before their benefit award is calculated.
  • Paragraph 1(c) [The Emergency Alarm System Excluder]: The precise clause within Schedule 1 that blocks public benefit funding for personal safety equipment, pull-cords, wearable buttons, lifelines, or daily check-in alarms.
  • Paragraph 1(f) [The Support Services Excluder]: The precise clause within Schedule 1 that blocks public funding for personnel costs tied to checking on a tenant's health, general counselling, well-being calls, or personal lifestyle support.
  • Paragraph 1(g) [The Accommodation Nexus Excluder]: The underlying baseline legal rule of English welfare benefits. It dictates that for any service charge to be covered by public money, it must have a direct connection (nexus) to maintaining the physical building. If a fee only benefits the person rather than repairing or cleaning the property structure, it is legally excluded.
  • Universal Credit (Housing Costs Element): The modern, consolidated national welfare benefit managed by central government that is steadily replacing legacy Housing Benefit. Its rules regarding what service charges can be covered are tightly locked into four distinct building management boxes (communal maintenance, communal cleaning, shared utility lifts, and basic eligible administration).

2. Local Government & Research Jargon

  • Local Billing Authority (Local Authority / LA): A lower-tier municipal government body—such as a District, Borough, or City Council—that is legally responsible for collection of council taxes and the direct day-to-day administration and payout of local housing benefit claims.
  • Upper-Tier Authority (County Council): A higher-level regional government tier responsible for wider public infrastructures like highways, schools, and social care budgets. They hold zero legal jurisdiction or data records regarding housing rent benefits.
  • The Postcode Lottery: A term used to describe the unfair scenario where identical technology hardware or tenant services are funded by one local authority but strictly rejected by a neighboring council due to inconsistent interpretations of the exact same national welfare law.
  • Freedom of Information (FOI) Act 2000: The statutory legal mechanism used to conduct this research. It gives members of the public a legal right to request and receive recorded operational data, policy documents, and decision records held by public sector organizations.
  • Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO): The independent national regulator and enforcement body responsible for upholding information rights, data privacy laws, and supervising public bodies to ensure they comply with their statutory FOI duties.
  • Internal Review: A formal administrative appeal process where a researcher or citizen forces a public body to independently re-examine how they handled an information request, used to challenge wrongful delays or non-compliant answers.

3. Operational & Technological Housing Terms

  • Intensive Housing Management (IHM): A specialized category of allowable landlord management tasks that can legally be paid for by public housing benefits. To qualify as IHM, a task must focus strictly on building upkeep, such as processing rapid structural repair demands, logging maintenance patterns, or conducting building occupancy checks to prevent tenancy fraud.
  • Personal Care and Support: Any action or service designed to check on, comfort, or monitor the personal health and safety of an individual tenant, rather than maintaining the brick-and-mortar property. This is strictly prohibited from being included in a rental benefit claim.
  • Cost-Splitting Apportionment Precedent: An analytical billing technique used by advanced benefits departments.   • Technology Enabled Care (TEC) / Telecare: A broad industry term covering digital support hardware, smart tablets, wrist sensors, fall detectors, and central monitoring networks designed to help older or vulnerable residents live independently.
  • Social Landlord / Registered Provider (RP): A non-profit housing association, local council landlord, or trust organization that provides subsidized, supported, or sheltered accommodation to individuals with distinct housing and welfare needs.
