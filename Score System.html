Score System

To keep things as simple as possible I devised a basic score system using a combination of

any three of the following: “YES”, “NO”, “N/A” or “AMBIGUOUS”.

·       “YES” A categorical statement or a response that could not reasonably be interpreted as any other answer.

·       “NO” A categorical statement or a response that could not reasonably be interpreted as any other answer.

·       “N/A” Where, for example, a respondent has no record at all for OKEachDay or a similar service or may have stated “N/A” or Not Applicable within their response.

·       “AMBIGUOUS” Where a statement or a response indicates that one or the other of the possible answers may prevail depending on the circumstance at the time.

 

Applying The Scores:

Holds Data:

Does the council hold any data on the OKEachDay Service or a similar service from another provider.

Possible answers: YES, NO, or N/A

 

 

OKEachDay or a similar service Allowed:

If they hold the data, did they say if the OKEachDay Service or a similar service from another provider was or would be considered as eligible for Housing Benefit and the reasons why.

 

For example, yes it was allowed or no it was not, or where a response stated that a service might be considered but further information would be requested from a claimant.

 

Within this category, some councils were able to provide how they would categorise OKEachDay or a similar service if they were to receive a Housing Benefit application that contained either.  This allowed a mark for those responses where a LA did not have specific records.

Possible answers: YES, NO, N/A or AMBIGUOUS

 

Other Ineligible Service:

Does the council provide its own in-house alarm or monitoring service, or that of a preferred third-party provider.

Possible answers: YES, NO, or N/A
