Chapter: Governing Legislation & Statutory Boundaries

This section examines the statutory frameworks that govern whether service charges can be included in rental accounts under English law. To ensure this report is suitable for a layperson, the analysis focuses on the specific regulations used by Local Authorities to evaluate daily contact, warden, and digital check-in systems.

1. The Statutory Framework

Two core pieces of legislation govern housing benefit administration in England:

  • The Housing Benefit Regulations 2006 (SI 2006/213): Applies to benefit claimants of working age.
  • The Housing Benefit (Persons who have attained the qualifying age for state pension credit) Regulations 2006 (SI 2006/214): Applies to claimants who have reached state pension credit age.

Under both sets of regulations, an allowable service charge must be directly connected to the provision, maintenance, and utility management of the physical building ("adequate accommodation"). If a charge relates to personal support, care, or daily lifestyle convenience, it is deemed ineligible and cannot be funded using public welfare budgets.

2. Analysis of Schedule 1: Ineligible Service Charges

Schedule 1, Paragraph 1 of the regulations explicitly lists the services that Local Authorities are legally prohibited from funding. Daily-contact systems like OKEachDay trigger several distinct restriction markers within this schedule:

Sub-paragraph (c): Emergency Alarm Systems

  • Statutory Text: Prohibits "charges in respect of the provision of an emergency alarm system".
  • Application: Daily welfare check-ins, automated telephone call alerts, and digital response triggers are categorized by most authorities alongside analogue pull-cords or warden alarms. These features provide a safety net for the tenant rather than maintaining the building infrastructure, making them ineligible for standard housing benefit coverage.

Sub-paragraph (f): General Counselling and Support Services

  • Statutory Text: Prohibits "charges in respect of general counselling or of any other support services, whoever provides those services".
